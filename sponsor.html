Questions sponsors usually ask

Frequently asked questions

What does a sponsored video on Mylemans Online cost?

Pricing depends on the format, the product and the scope of the integration, so I don't publish a rate card. Send a sponsorship request with your product and goals and you'll have a quote within two business days.

What audience does Mylemans Online reach?

IT professionals and serious hobbyists: about 80% of viewers are 25–44. The largest audience is in the United States, followed by Europe (Germany and the UK lead), India and Canada. About 40% of views come from YouTube search and roughly six in ten go to videos more than a year old, so an integration keeps being seen long after launch week. Viewers are decision-makers or influencers for their own infrastructure and often for their workplace.

Can I see examples of past sponsored content?

Yes. The Past partnerships section above links straight to the videos and posts, plus two evergreen tutorials. Audience figures and performance data for comparable sponsorships are shared on request as part of the quote.

What's the typical turnaround time for a sponsored video?

For an integrated segment inside a planned tutorial: typically 3–4 weeks from agreement to publication. For a dedicated video (production + placement): typically 6–8 weeks including setup, testing, scripting, recording, editing, and publication. Rush turnarounds are possible for an additional fee.

What kinds of products fit best with the channel?

Products that solve real problems for homelab operators, prosumers, and IT professionals running their own infrastructure. Strong fits include: virtualization platforms, backup and storage tools, networking hardware (switches, routers, firewalls), remote access and tunneling services, monitoring and observability tools, security software, SaaS for sysadmins, and hosting providers. The common thread is that the audience uses these products themselves, not for someone else.

Are there products you won't promote?

Yes. I don't promote products I haven't tested in a real environment. I don't read scripts I didn't write or didn't agree to. I don't make competitive comparisons that misrepresent other vendors. I don't claim performance, security, or compliance benefits I haven't verified. I don't accept performance-only or affiliate-only deals as the primary compensation structure. And I won't take sponsorships for product categories that conflict with the audience's interests (e.g., consumer crypto, gambling, content unrelated to IT infrastructure). These constraints make the work better for both sides, in my experience.
